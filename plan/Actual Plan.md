## Train the hybrid YOLOv8s (The Dense Detector)
We modify standard YOLOv8 to process the high-resolution crops generated by the Crop & Route logic. This phase uses hybrid precision: extreme binary quantization for the heavy spatial processing, and Float32 for the deep semantic reasoning.

### Data Preparation (The Crops)
- **Input:** Take the 640x640 Regions of Interest (RoIs) isolated by Phase 2 from the unscaled drone image.
- **Bit-Plane Extraction:** Extract **all 8 bit-planes** from the crop (e.g., from grayscale). Unlike the heatmap, we *must* include the LSBs here. The MSBs gave us the shape of the crop, but the LSBs contain the subtle sub-pixel contrast required to actually detect the tiny 15px pedestrians inside it. 
- **Packing:** These 8 planes pack perfectly into a single `uint8` per pixel, matching the input format of the Phase 2 C-kernel.

### Architecture Modifications (YOLO Surgery)
Open the `yolov8s.yaml` configuration and apply the following constraints:
- **The Binary Backbone (High-Res Layers):** Replace the first 4-5 `Conv` and `C2f` blocks with our custom `BinaryConv` module. During training, this layer uses the PyTorch Straight-Through Estimator (STE) wrapper to simulate `+1` and `-1` math.
- **The 64-Channel Rule:** We must force the `out_channels` of every binary layer to be strict multiples of 64 (e.g., 64, 128, 256). This is non-negotiable. It guarantees that during deployment, the depth of every pixel maps perfectly into 1, 2, or 4 `uint64` CPU registers for the POPCNT instruction.
- **The FP32 Neck & Head (Low-Res Layers):** Once the spatial resolution downsamples to 80x80 or 40x40, revert to standard Float32 `Conv` layers. At these tiny resolutions, FP32 is computationally trivial for the CPU. Furthermore, the detection head *must* exit the binary domain to calculate precise, continuous bounding box coordinates and class probabilities.

### Training Strategy
- **Dataset:** Train the network strictly on the 640x640 crops containing ground-truth labels, completely ignoring the empty 1920x1080 background space.
- **Loss:** Use standard YOLOv8 loss functions (CIoU for bounding box regression, DFL, and BCE for classification). 
- **Optimization:** The STE wrapper will handle the quantization-aware training. The loss gradients will flow backward through the FP32 head, bypass the `sign()` functions in the binary backbone, and update the "hidden" continuous weights.

### Deployment (The Transition to C)
- Once training converges, the PyTorch model's job is done.
- We freeze the weights, extract the `+1` and `-1` values from the `BinaryConv` layers, and bit-pack them into `.bin` files.
- During live inference on the drone, the Python framework is discarded. The crops are fed into the C-engine, where the `uint64` registers and `POPCNT` commands execute the backbone at 60+ FPS, handing the final tiny feature maps off to a lightweight FP32 head.
